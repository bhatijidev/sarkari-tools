Understanding US Mortgage Payments (PITI)

When buying a home in the United States, your monthly housing expense consists of more than just principal and interest. Lenders calculate your total monthly obligation using PITI:

  • Principal: The portion of your payment that directly reduces your outstanding loan balance.
  • Interest: The lender's charge for borrowing money, calculated based on your annual percentage rate (APR).
  • Taxes: Annual municipal and county property taxes, typically escrowed by your lender and paid in monthly installments.
  • Insurance: Homeowners property and hazard insurance covering damage, fire, or natural disasters.
  • PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance): Required on conventional loans with down payments less than 20% to protect the lender against default.

Standard US Mortgage Loan Types

  • 30-Year Fixed Mortgage: The most popular loan in America. It offers the lowest monthly payments with predictable, locked-in interest rates for 360 months.
  • 15-Year Fixed Mortgage: Offers lower interest rates than 30-year loans and allows you to pay off your home in half the time, saving tens of thousands in interest at the expense of a higher monthly payment.
  • FHA Loans: Federal Housing Administration backed loans allowing down payments as low as 3.5%, though requiring upfront and annual Mortgage Insurance Premiums (MIP).
  • VA Loans: Guaranteed by the Department of Veterans Affairs for eligible US military service members with 0% down payment and zero monthly PMI.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 28/36 rule in US mortgage qualification?
The 28/36 rule is a standard debt-to-income benchmark used by US mortgage underwriters. It states that your total monthly housing expenses (PITI) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, and total debt payments (mortgage, auto loans, student loans, credit cards) should not exceed 36%.
How can I cancel PMI on my mortgage?
Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act, you have the right to request PMI cancellation once your loan principal balance reaches 80% of your home's original appraised value. Lenders are legally required to automatically terminate PMI when your balance reaches 78%.
Does making bi-weekly payments pay off the loan faster?
Yes! By paying half your mortgage payment every two weeks instead of once a month, you make 26 half-payments a year — equivalent to 13 full monthly payments. This extra annual payment can shave 4 to 6 years off a 30-year mortgage without stressing your monthly budget.
